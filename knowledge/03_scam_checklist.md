# Legitimate Trader vs Scam — Checklist

## Stop immediately if any of these happen
- [ ] They promise **guaranteed**, "risk-free" or steady returns (for example "2% a day").
- [ ] They want you to **deposit money** into their platform, app, "AI bot" or "managed account".
- [ ] They move you to **WhatsApp, Telegram or Discord groups** where "mentors" or "professors" give tips.
- [ ] Your dashboard shows big gains, but **withdrawing is hard**, or needs a "fee" or "tax" first.
- [ ] They pressure you: "limited spots", "act today".
- [ ] They aren't registered, or you can't verify who they are.
- [ ] They use celebrity videos or voices. These can be AI deepfakes.

**Evidence:**
- The SEC, NASAA and FINRA have warned that fraudsters use AI hype, deepfakes and fake AI trading platforms showing fabricated gains while keeping deposits. [FINRA: AI and Investment Fraud](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/artificial-intelligence-and-investment-fraud) · [NASAA advisory on fake "AI bots" and "investment education foundations"](https://www.nasaa.org/75050/informed-investor-advisory-crypto-scams-investment-education-ai-bots/)
- The SEC charged operators of a $14 million scheme using fake AI tips in WhatsApp "investment clubs" and fake crypto platforms. [SEC press release 2025-144](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025-144-sec-charges-three-purported-crypto-asset-trading-platforms-four-investment-clubs-scheme-targeted)

## Signs of a legitimate teacher
- [ ] You can **verify their track record**: audited results, a regulated fund history, live account reports, or contest results.
- [ ] They **show losses and drawdowns**, not just wins.
- [ ] They talk more about **risk** (position size, stop losses, diversification) than about profits.
- [ ] They say most people lose money, and explain why.
- [ ] They **never ask you to send them money to trade**. Teaching or selling books is different from holding your funds.
- [ ] Their claims are modest: for example 10–20% a year with large drawdowns possible, not 10% a week.

## How to check someone
1. Search their name together with "scam", "SEC" and "lawsuit".
2. Check whether they are registered: [FINRA BrokerCheck](https://brokercheck.finra.org/) (brokers and advisers), [SEC IAPD](https://adviserinfo.sec.gov/), or [NFA BASIC](https://www.nfa.futures.org/BasicNet/) (futures).
3. Ask how they make money. Mostly from selling courses and signals rather than from trading is a yellow flag.
4. Look for independent reviews, not the testimonials on their own site.
