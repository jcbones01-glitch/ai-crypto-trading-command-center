# What Legitimate Traders Agree On

These are the creator's synthesis of the teachers in [02_legitimate_teachers.md](02_legitimate_teachers.md) and the evidence in [01_evidence_on_trading.md](01_evidence_on_trading.md). They are **principles, not guarantees.**

1. **Survive first.** Risk only a small part of your account on any one trade; about 1% or less is common advice. Losing streaks are guaranteed, so the goal is to still be in the game afterward.
2. **Costs decide everything.** Fees, spreads and slippage eat short-term strategies. The shorter the holding time, the bigger the edge you need.
3. **Diversify.** Many markets and many small bets beat one big bet. (Carver, Clenow)
4. **Trends are the most widely documented edge for systematic traders.** It works over weeks to months, not minutes, and has long, painful losing periods. (Clenow, Carver)
5. **Have rules and follow them.** Write the strategy down before trading it. Emotional, impulsive trades are where most retail losses come from.
6. **Test honestly.**
   - Test on data the strategy has never seen.
   - Count how many ideas you tried.
   - Stress-test the strategy, for example with Monte Carlo (Davey).
   - Then paper trade it before using real money.
7. **Expect modest returns and real drawdowns.** Good professional systems target roughly 10–20% a year and can fall 20–30% or more along the way. Anyone showing smooth, huge returns is suspect.
8. **Start small with real money.** Only after paper trading works, and only with money you can afford to lose.
9. **The index fund is the benchmark.** If a strategy can't beat low-cost index investing after costs and risk, just buy the index. (SPIVA)
10. **AI is a tool, not magic.** Machine learning helps with research and automation, but it overfits easily. Most AI trading products sold to the public are marketing, or scams.
