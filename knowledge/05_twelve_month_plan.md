# 12-Month Plan: Learn, Paper Trade, Then Decide

Built for an owner with **no trading capital for about 12 months**, whose future capital will come from job income. Every step that involves building anything needs the owner's approval.

## Months 1–3: Learn the basics (free)
- Read Rob Carver's free blog, starting with the "Start here" page.
- Borrow *Systematic Trading* (Carver) through a library app.
- Learn with the creator (Claude): ask questions in plain language, and the creator adds what you learn to this folder.
- **Creator proposal (needs approval):** a simple paper-trading setup. For example, a free [Alpaca paper account](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/paper-trading) (a $100k virtual balance; paper keys cannot touch real money) running one simple, well-known strategy such as monthly trend following on a few ETFs. The aim is to learn how automation works, not to find a secret edge.

## Months 4–6: Paper trade and keep score
- The paper strategy runs automatically. The creator writes a weekly or monthly report: return vs an S&P 500 index fund, the largest drop (drawdown), and number of trades.
- Log every change and every idea tried.
- ~~Optional: test one short-term idea (the H-ST1 draft) on historical data.~~ Shelved by the owner on 2026-09-28; only if the owner reopens it.

## Months 7–9: Evaluate honestly
- Compare the paper results with simply holding an index fund.
- Keep only what beat the index after costs **and** was tolerable during its worst drop.
- Expect most ideas to be killed. That is success, because it's free.

## Months 10–12: Get financially ready (when job income starts)
1. Build an emergency fund of 3–6 months of expenses in savings.
2. Start automatic investing in a low-cost broad index fund. This is the reliable part of the plan.
3. **Only if** a paper strategy passed months 4–9: consider a *small* real-money trial with 5% or less of savings, no leverage and no borrowing, with the owner's written approval.

## Before opening any real accounts
- Get a government photo ID. Some banks use screening services such as ChexSystems, and credit unions can be more flexible. See [First Step Alliance banking guide](https://www.firststepalliance.org/post/guide-to-banking-returning-citizens).
- If you're under any supervision conditions, confirm there are no restrictions on financial accounts.
