Selected per-slot rejection rates under null cases also show that the problem
is not merely one isolated family event. In `heteroskedastic_null`, for
example, BTC DEP rejected in 12.7% of replications, ETH DEP in 11.2%, BTC TIME
in 9.4%, and ETH TIME in 8.8%. The fixed six-slot Holm procedure therefore did
not achieve the preregistered family-level engineering bound under the
registered synthetic conditions.

## Interpretation

The synthetic core demonstrates high detection power for the planted stable,
time-varying, and state-varying dependence alternatives, but the current
asymptotic HAC/Wald inference is not sufficiently calibrated under the frozen
synthetic nulls.

This is a statistical-calibration failure, not evidence about BTC, ETH,
Adaptive Markets, or tradability. It is exactly the type of false-positive
risk the synthetic gate was designed to detect before empirical inspection.

The bid/ask-bounce case also behaves as intended as a caution: the procedure
detects dependence generated by a measurement/microstructure mechanism even
though the latent efficient-price innovations are independent. Detection of
serial dependence must therefore not be equated with an economic trading edge.

## Decision

**EMPIRICAL RELEASE REMAINS BLOCKED.**

Do not run `AMS-DEP-001-v1` on actual BTCUSDT/ETHUSDT returns.

Do not:

- weaken the 0.075 null-family ceiling;
- remove difficult null cases;
- reroll seeds;
- reduce the six-test multiplicity family;
- reinterpret the failed screen as acceptable because power is high;
- access Validation or locked OOS;
- generate strategy P&L or trading signals.

Any revised inference method is a new, prospectively specified calibration
version. The V1 result and artifact must remain retained. A future version
must state the statistical rationale for the change before new synthetic
results are observed and must pass its own fixed calibration gate before any
empirical-release review.
